# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Design-conscious founders, brand directors, and creative leaders evaluating a premium studio for a distinctive digital flagship. They arrive expecting visual proof of taste, technical confidence, and a clear path to commission work.

## Product Purpose

The Site Library presents a digital design studio through the metaphor of a private publishing archive. Each commissioned site becomes a bound volume. The experience must make the studio's process, portfolio, and positioning tangible through one spatial narrative: a book travels into the library, joins the shelf, becomes portfolio navigation, and leaves one future place for the next client.

Success means the visitor understands that the studio creates authored, lasting digital work rather than interchangeable templates, can inspect every selected project, and can commission a site without the cinematic layer obstructing access.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, tactile, exacting.

The voice is editorial and assured. It communicates luxury through proportion, material, typography, and restraint rather than decoration or status language. The emotional arc moves from curiosity, to spatial discovery, to confidence, to invitation.

## Anti-references

- Standard agency portfolios with a decorative 3D hero above a card grid.
- Literal Victorian, gothic, fantasy, Hogwarts, steampunk, or nostalgic old-book imagery.
- Generic black luxury websites, neon technology demos, futuristic HUDs, glassmorphism, and floating gradient blobs.
- Stripe Press imitation, copied cover systems, or borrowed Stripe branding.
- Three.js spectacle disconnected from navigation or story.
- Template-driven SaaS hierarchy, excessive rounded cards, and gratuitous scroll effects.

## Design Principles

1. Make the metaphor functional. Every portfolio project is a book, and every book is a usable navigation target.
2. Reveal one continuous place. Scroll moves through a persistent library instead of switching between unrelated scenes.
3. Treat motion as physical editing. Camera, light, typography, and object movement clarify the narrative and reverse deterministically.
4. Let materials prove craft. Linen, paper, walnut, limestone, and restrained foil must read through geometry, light, and surface response.
5. Preserve the full story without WebGL. Semantic HTML, keyboard controls, reduced motion, and a static fallback are first-class versions of the same content.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, screen-reader project controls, touch-first project selection, 200% zoom, reduced motion, high-contrast readable copy, a reachable footer, and a complete WebGL fallback. The cinematic experience must never trap scroll or make hover the only path to content.
